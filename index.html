How the calculator works

It uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation to estimate your BMR (calories burned at rest), then multiplies by your activity level to get maintenance calories. For fat loss we subtract about 400 kcal, and for muscle gain we add about 300 kcal.

  • Protein: 1.8 g per kg (2.0 g per kg when cutting) to protect and build muscle.
  • Fats: 25% of calories for hormones and health.
  • Carbs: the remaining calories, which fuel your training.
FAQ

Questions? Answered.

How many calories should I eat per day?

It depends on your age, height, weight, gender and activity. Use the calculator for maintenance calories, then subtract about 400 kcal to lose fat or add about 300 kcal to gain muscle.

How much protein do I need to build muscle?

Most lifters do well with 1.6 to 2.2 g of protein per kg of body weight daily.

Is this calculator accurate?

It is a good starting estimate based on the Mifflin-St Jeor equation. Track your weight for 2-3 weeks and adjust by 100-200 kcal if needed.

Can I hit my macros with desi food?

Yes. Daal, roti, chicken, eggs, dahi and milk can cover your protein, carbs and fats on a student budget. See the desi nutrition guide for exact numbers.
